## The TA's topic description (original text, Chinese)
研究模型能從音樂的哪些部分辨識年代與發行市場，以及這些線索能否用來控制翻唱歌曲的風格。初步想使用規模比 HW1 更大的 Discogs-VI 資料，先比較原曲、人聲與伴奏各自對分類結果的影響，並分析不同年代或市場最容易混淆的歌曲。這可以幫助我們判斷模型學到的究竟是演唱語言、編曲與錄音特色，還是資料分布造成的偏差。接著，如果找得到較穩定的年代風格線索，想嘗試讓同一首歌生成不同年代風格的 cover，檢查生成結果是否保留原曲的旋律，同時呈現目標年代的特色。發行市場標籤代表歌曲的發行地，不一定是藝人的出身地；這點也會納入分析。實際範圍可以依資料與時間再一起調整。

English gloss: study which parts of music let a model recognize the release era and release market, and whether those cues can control the style of cover songs. Use a larger Discogs-VI dataset than in HW1; compare original mix, vocals and accompaniment; analyze which eras or markets (and which songs) are most confused; decide whether the model learns singing language, arrangement and recording style, or dataset bias. If stable era cues exist, generate covers of the same song in different eras, check that the melody is kept and the target era's traits appear. The market label is the place of release, not necessarily the artist's origin, and this should be part of the analysis.

## What has already been done in the project (so you can skip the basics)
- Data: the course's PA1 subset of Discogs-VI (30 s clips at 24 kHz; dataset A: 6 decades, 1026 train and 132 validation labeled clips; dataset B: 6 release markets in the 1980s, 798 train and 102 validation labeled clips; splits are artist-disjoint by the organizers; the manifest has no artist, genre, country or year columns).
- Stem analysis: HTDemucs vocal/accompaniment separation (second separator BS-RoFormer as a check), frozen-encoder linear probes (MERT-v2, MuQ, Whisper, AST, CLAP) on mix, vocals and accompaniment; language-ID baseline; vocal-bleed check; harmonic/percussive split; production-only controls.
- Generation: MusicGen-Melody with era prompts on the accompaniment while keeping the original vocal, a LoRA fine-tune of MusicGen-Melody on era accompaniment, MuseControlLite on Stable Audio Open as a second generator; evaluation with codec-matched era probes, song identification by chroma DTW, chord agreement, KAD, Audiobox Aesthetics, COCOLA.

## What I need (answer each numbered item; for every item give: (i) the current best systems and their reported numbers, (ii) the bottleneck or failure mode the authors name, (iii) the future-work items they list, (iv) the datasets and splits they use, with size, labels, license and audio availability)

1. **Era / decade / release-year prediction from audio.** Best systems and numbers (accuracy, within-N-years, MAE), the encoders used (MERT, MuQ, CLAP, MAEST, Essentia Discogs-EffNet and others), how label noise from re-releases and compilations is handled, and what the authors say limits performance (reissues, remastering, loudness war, genre confounds). Include regression or ordinal formulations.
2. **Release-market / country / region prediction from audio.** Same questions. Distinguish release country, artist origin and language. Is there any work on predicting country of release, and how often do the labels diverge from artist origin (Discogs and MusicBrainz release-country semantics, regional pressings, imports)? What features carry market: language, instrumentation, tuning and scales, mastering practice, mono/stereo, label practice?
3. **Discogs-VI and larger alternatives.** Exact content of Discogs-VI and its audio companion (Discogs-VI-YT, YouTube-linked), versions, size, which metadata fields exist (artist, genre/style, country, year, release id, master id, version groups), what the MIREX-style splits and clique structure allow, license and redistribution limits, and how a team without the course audio could rebuild a larger subset legally. Alternatives with year and country metadata (MusicBrainz and AcousticBrainz derivatives, Million Song Dataset derivatives, MTG-Jamendo, FMA, Free Music Archive, Da-TACOS, SHS100K, Discogs-based sets, DALI, any dataset with release country). Recommend what is realistic within about a week and what scale is needed for stable per-class estimates.
4. **Stem-wise attribution (mix vs vocals vs accompaniment).** Papers that separate sources to test what a classifier uses (genre, mood, singer, language, instrument, era, region). Known confounds: separator artifacts and bleed, bandwidth and mono/stereo differences, loudness normalization. Recommended control experiments and statistics (paired tests, bootstrap, nested comparisons, ablation by source).
5. **Language vs arrangement/recording vs dataset bias.** Work on shortcut learning and confounds in music classification: artist and album effects, production/mastering fingerprints, language as a proxy for region, genre confounds for era, sampling-rate and codec artifacts. Controls proposed in the literature (instrumental-only subsets, language ID as covariate, artist-disjoint and album-disjoint splits, adversarial removal, probing with control tasks, MDL probing, causal or counterfactual tests, concept erasure such as INLP or LEACE applied to audio embeddings). Which of these are feasible on frozen embeddings only?
6. **Which songs and classes are confused.** Methods for analyzing confusion at the song level and class level: ordinal error structure, hard-example mining, annotation of confused songs (re-releases, cover versions, retro or revival styles, genre outliers), data-map or training-dynamics analyses, influence functions. What is known about why adjacent decades or neighboring markets are confused?
7. **Cover / restyle generation that keeps the melody.** Systems for era-, genre- or timbre-style transfer that preserve melody and lyrics: MusicGen-Melody, Stable Audio Open and ControlNet-style adapters (Music ControlNet, MuseControlLite), audio-to-audio diffusion and SDEdit-style editing (AudioLDM2, MusicMagus, ZETA, DDPM-inversion), Stable Audio 2/2.5 audio-to-audio, ACE-Step and other open song generators, stem-aware accompaniment generation (SingSong, StemGen, MusicGen-Stem, FastSAG, Diff-A-Riff, JASCO), singing-voice conversion for cover generation, lyric-preserving cover work, LoRA or adapter fine-tuning for style. For each: inputs, what is kept, what changes, open weights, compute, quality caveats, and the failure modes authors report (melody drift, rhythm drift, vocal-accompaniment mismatch, mixing artifacts).
8. **Era as a controllable attribute in generation.** Does any work condition generation on release year or decade (text prompts with decade, year tokens, metadata conditioning, production-style or mastering-chain conditioning, audio-effects or "vintage" processing, neural audio effects, style embeddings)? How do they evaluate that the era is actually present? Include work on automatic mixing and mastering style transfer (Diff-MST, Fx-Encoder, ST-ITO) as era-production controls.
9. **Evaluation of melody preservation and target-style presence.** Metrics and protocols used in recent papers: chroma / CQT DTW, melody extraction agreement (RPA, RCA), cover-song-identification embeddings (CoverHunter, Re-MOVE, ByteCover), chord and beat agreement, CLAP score, FAD and KAD, MuLan or CLAP style-retrieval scores, classifier-based evaluators and their circularity, MOS and A/B listening tests (sample sizes, designs). Which are validated against human judgments?
10. **Novelty check and positioning.** Has anyone (a) analyzed era or market classification stem by stem, (b) used a probe or tagger as a generation evaluator for era, (c) fine-tuned a generator on era-labeled accompaniment? State what appears new and what has been done, without overclaiming.
11. **Limits of frozen-encoder probing.** Known issues with linear probes on music encoders (layer choice, probe capacity, selectivity), comparison of MERT, MuQ, MAEST, CLAP, Whisper and Discogs-EffNet on region and era tasks, and fine-tuning vs probing evidence.
12. **Future-work lists.** From every paper above, collect the "limitations" and "future work" sentences (quote or tightly paraphrase, with section reference). Group them into themes (data scale, label quality, controls, generation fidelity, evaluation) and say which of them a five-week student project could address.
