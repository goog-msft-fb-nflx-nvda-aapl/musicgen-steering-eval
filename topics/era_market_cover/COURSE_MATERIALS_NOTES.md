# Course lectures read for topic 2: what we can reuse

Read 2026-10-25 from the course PDFs (text extracted, slide titles and references only; no external lookup). Lecture names are the PDF file names. Dates in the guideline PDFs are a stale "2025 edition" and ignored (see project notes).

## Directly usable
| Lecture | What it gives us | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| `lecture11b_covergen_chihpin` (Chih-Pin Tan, cover generation) | Formulation: cover generation = **content-preserving style transfer**; content = melody + chords, style = everything else. Pipelines: audio to MIDI to cover (Pop2Piano with MT3, PiCoGen/PiCoGen2 with SheetSage), audio to audio (SongEcho on ACE-Step). States that evaluation of cover generation is "a mess": style is ill-defined, similarity is not the same as a good cover, metrics borrowed from general generation are unsuited. Lists datasets (Discogs-VI, LargeSHS/SecondHandSongs, POP909, Pop2Piano) and invites students working on cover generation to contact the lecturer. | Adopt his content/style definition in the report; our identification score (content) plus separate style presence (era) follows it; cite his evaluation critique to justify a validated, control-based metric suite; Discogs-VI cliques and SecondHandSongs are the source of real covers for reference-based evaluation; the lecturer is a possible contact for feedback. |
| `DeepMIR-AutoMixing` (Yen-Tung Yeh) | Mixing = chain of audio effects (gain, EQ, compression, reverb); mixing is the inverse of separation; differentiable mixing console (DMC: controller + transformation network); differentiable signal processing; **FX normalization** (remove average effects per stem type); **Diff-MST** (differentiable mixing style transfer, ISMIR 2024); **Fx-Encoder++** (instrument-wise effects representations from mixtures, ISMIR 2025); CLAP used to relate audio and text descriptions; limited mixing datasets. | (1) Era is largely a production/mixing property, so an effects-aware embedding (Fx-Encoder++) is a candidate era or production metric that is independent of musical content and of the codec-artifact problem of semantic encoders (to check: availability of checkpoints). (2) Our crude DSP counterfactual (band-limit, saturation) should become a principled differentiable or parametric chain (EQ, compressor, reverb) for the "production-only" control. (3) Diff-MST style transfer is an interpretable non-generative baseline: transfer the mixing style of a real era reference onto the accompaniment stems and compare with MusicGen regeneration. (4) FX normalization is the analogue of our loudness/bandwidth normalization controls. |
| `lecture03b_separation_short` | Separation is conditional audio generation; stems types (2, 4, beyond 4); HT Demucs v4 hybrid transformer; BS-RoFormer and model zoo (ZFTurbo checkpoints), Ultimate Vocal Remover (lead vs backing vocals); metrics SDR, SIR, SAR; phase problem. | We already use HT Demucs; add SDR/SIR/SAR language to the bleed discussion (needs ground-truth stems, which we lack, so only a proxy); UVR could split lead from backing vocals to test whether market information sits in lead vocals; BS-RoFormer as a second separator to test separator-dependence of the stem results. |
| `lecture13_transcription` | F0 estimation (YIN, pYIN, CREPE, deep salience), polyphonic transcription (Onsets and Frames, basic-pitch, MT3, Omnizart), melody transcription by generative pre-training (SheetSage, ISMIR 2022), chord/beat models. | Melody and F0 extraction for the content metrics (RPA/RCA with mir_eval on the accompaniment lead line, basic-pitch or CREPE), SheetSage-style melody for a symbolic content comparison, chord recognition instead of our template matching. |
| `lecture05_text-to-music_transformer` | VQ-VAE, SoundStream, EnCodec, DAC (RVQ codecs), MusicLM, MusicGen family; "Codec does matter: exploring the semantic shortcoming of codec for audio language models" (Ye et al. 2024). | Supports the codec-matched evaluation design: real references go through the same EnCodec path (MusicGen's 32 kHz compression model); the codec-shortcoming paper is a citable motivation. |
| `lecture06_text-to-music_diffusion` | DDPM/DDIM, **classifier-free guidance**, latent diffusion, ControlNet, AudioLDM 2, Stable Audio (and Open), DAC-style autoencoder. | Guidance scale is a standard knob: we only used 3.0, so add a guidance sweep (1, 3, 5, 7) as an ablation for style strength vs content preservation. Stable Audio Open plus MuseControlLite (below) is a second generator. |
| `lecture03_language-and-music` | Music captioning (LP-MusicCaps, MusicCaps), audio-language models (LLARK, MuChoMusic benchmark), joint embeddings **MuLan, CLAP**, **MuseControlLite (the instructor group's ICML 2025 paper, slide 34)**, "Live music models". | MuseControlLite (conditions on melody, rhythm, dynamics, text on Stable Audio Open) is the in-course alternative to MusicGen-Melody and gets the instructor's attention; CLAP/MuLan for text-audio era alignment (unvalidated per our survey: test it with the positive control); LP-MusicCaps-style LLM captions could produce richer era prompts than our one-line prompts. |

## Possible extensions
- `final_project_ideas`, item 5 (explainability): sparse autoencoders on audio latents and RFM steering of MusicGen. A sparse autoencoder on MERT/MuQ stem embeddings could show whether interpretable features carry era or language, a natural second analysis beyond probes. (Item 2, copyright and training-data attribution, is a different topic.)
- `lecture12_miscellaneous`: music structure analysis (Buisson et al.) and audio-to-audio alignment/score following (Arzt, Fremerey, Grachten): alignment tools for comparing a cover with its original beyond plain DTW; structure agreement as an extra content metric.
- `lecture02_classification` (PA1 notes): probe protocols already used.

## Not relevant for this topic
`lecture07_singing_generation` (SVS; only relevant if we replaced the kept vocal), `lecture09/10_midi_generation`, `lecture11_midi-to-audio` (WaveNet, GANSynth, instrument synthesis), `lecture01b_fundamentals_audio` (basic signal processing, useful for the loudness/bandwidth controls), `HW3`/`Hw1`.

## Final project requirements from `final_project_guidelines`
Oral presentation of 8 minutes focused on methodology, results and findings, with **audio demos required for analysis projects too**; paper report in the ISMIR template with an appendix of individual contributions; code upload to GitHub with an open-source license is encouraged. Practical consequence: we need a small demo set of generated clips (the course data license allows course use; keep demo audio out of the public repo).

## Suggested uses, in order
1. Cite lecture 11b's content/style definition and evaluation critique in the report; align the metric names with it.
2. Replace the crude DSP control with a parametric effects chain (EQ, compressor, reverb), and check Fx-Encoder++/Diff-MST availability as an era production metric and a non-generative baseline.
3. Add a classifier-free-guidance sweep to the MusicGen-Melody runs (lecture 06) and consider MuseControlLite as second generator (lecture 03).
4. Use basic-pitch/CREPE (lecture 13) for melody metrics; BS-RoFormer or UVR (lecture 03b) for separator-dependence tests.
