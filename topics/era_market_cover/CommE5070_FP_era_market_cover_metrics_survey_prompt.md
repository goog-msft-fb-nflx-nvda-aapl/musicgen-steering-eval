You are helping with a graduate course final project (Deep Learning for Music Analysis and Generation, NTU, music generation track). I need a literature survey on **quantitative metrics**, with a recommendation I can implement in about one week. Be rigorous: every paper, metric, library and dataset must be real and carry a link (arXiv id, DOI, repository). If you cannot verify something, write "unverified". Do not invent numbers. ## The project
Two linked parts on 30 s music clips:
1. **Analysis.** Given clips labeled with release decade (6 classes, 1960s to 2010s) or release market (6 countries, all 1980s), measure how much the label information sits in the vocals, the accompaniment, or the full mix (HT Demucs separation, frozen MERT-v2 and MuQ embeddings, logistic-regression probes, paired McNemar tests), and test shortcuts (signal statistics, language identification, loudness, bandwidth).
2. **Generation.** Regenerate the accompaniment with MusicGen-Melody (chroma of the original mix as melody condition, text prompt naming a target era), keep the original vocal stem, remix. Goal: the cover keeps the song (melody, harmony, vocal) and shows the target era's style.

## What I have and what went wrong (so the survey targets real problems)
- Era presence is currently measured with a decade probe trained on real accompaniment stems. On generated audio it is domain-shifted: every generated clip, even with a neutral prompt, is predicted as the 2000s. Two encoders disagree on the prompt ordering (MuQ predicts the 1980s prompt as older than the 1960s prompt), and the MuQ probe moves strongly under a crude band-limit-plus-saturation filter that MERT ignores. So a probe score alone is not trustworthy as an era metric.
- Melody preservation is measured with chroma-CQT cosine between the original mix and the generated remix. A control that pairs the remix with a different song's mix already scores 0.63 to 0.77 (0.78 to 0.88 with DTW), so the metric has a high floor.
- Data are small: 12 test songs for generation, validation sets of 102 to 132 clips for probes. Sampling is stochastic (2 seeds per song and prompt).
- Not available: a trained cover-song identification model I trust, human ratings, real versions of the same song in the target era (maybe later via Discogs-VI cliques).

## Questions (answer each, numbered)

1. **Era or style presence in generated music.** Which quantitative metrics exist for "does this audio exhibit style or era X"? Cover: classifier-based scores and their known failure under domain shift; embedding-distance metrics against reference sets of real era music (Frechet Audio Distance and Kernel Audio Distance with different embeddings, FAD-infinity, per-song FAD), CLAP and text-audio scores for era prompts (is CLAP era alignment validated anywhere?), retrieval-based metrics (nearest real-era neighbors, kNN era vote in an embedding space), music-tagging models trained on Discogs styles or years (for example Essentia/MAEST style models), production-feature metrics (loudness, spectral tilt, dynamic range, bandwidth, noise floor) with era trends from the literature, and style-transfer metrics from audio or image style transfer that carry over. For each: what it measures, reference data needed, sample-size behavior (bias at small n), known failure modes, code availability, and evidence that it correlates with human judgment. Say which are suitable for n of about 12 songs by 3 eras by few seeds.
2. **Fixing the domain-shift problem.** Survey ways to make a probe or distance metric valid on generated audio: calibrating against a neutral-prompt baseline, training the probe on generated or codec-resynthesized audio of known era (for example passing real era recordings through the same codec such as EnCodec so real and generated share artifacts), anchoring against real-era references passed through the same pipeline, using relative (difference-in-differences) scores instead of absolute ones, adversarial or counterfactual controls (DSP-only "old" filters), and invariance to production. Which are established and which would be my own design?
3. **Content and melody preservation.** Metrics for "same song, different arrangement": chroma and CQT similarity with alignment (DTW, cross-similarity matrices), melody extraction accuracy (RMVPE, CREPE, basic-pitch; raw pitch and raw chroma accuracy), cover-song identification embeddings and distances (CLEWS, ByteCover family, CoverHunter, others) and how to calibrate them against real versions, chord-sequence agreement (chord recognition models), beat/tempo agreement, key agreement, structure agreement. Which have known floors or ceilings and how are they usually reported? How is melody preservation evaluated in MusicGen-Melody, MuseControlLite, Music ControlNet, SongEcho and related papers (list their metrics exactly)?
4. **Audio quality and prompt adherence.** Short list of the standard generation metrics (FAD with which embeddings, KL on tagger outputs, CLAP score, MuLan or similar, quality predictors such as Audiobox Aesthetics, MOS predictors), with the bias and sample-size caveats for tiny n, and which to use as secondary checks.
5. **Vocal and remix consistency.** Because the vocal stem is kept, is there anything to measure about the remix as a whole (vocal-accompaniment balance, key and tempo match between kept vocals and new accompaniment, timing alignment, masking)? Any metric for "does the new accompaniment fit the existing vocal"?
6. **Metrics for the analysis half.** Quantitative ways to attribute label information to stems beyond probe accuracy: accuracy drop under stem ablation or masking, Shapley or leave-one-stem-out attribution, information-theoretic measures (mutual information, MDL probing, minimum description length probes, control tasks with selectivity), calibration (log-loss, ECE), ordinal metrics for decade (mean absolute decade error, within-one accuracy, ordinal regression losses, quadratic weighted kappa), and effect sizes with confidence intervals for small validation sets (bootstrap, Bayesian comparison of classifiers, exact McNemar, correcting for multiple comparisons). Recommend a small standard set.
7. **Metric validation.** How do papers validate a new metric (correlation with human ratings, sensitivity to known manipulations, discriminative power, robustness across seeds)? Give a concrete low-cost meta-evaluation plan I can run without human listeners: for example (a) positive controls where the answer is known (real tracks of known decade passed through the codec; period-correct DSP chains), (b) negative controls (shuffled or wrong-era references, random audio), (c) sensitivity curves (how the metric moves as I morph the audio), (d) seed-to-seed variance. State the acceptance criteria that make a metric usable.
8. **Statistics for small n.** Recommended paired designs and tests for 12 songs by 3 conditions by seeds (mixed-effects models vs aggregating seeds then Wilcoxon, permutation tests, bootstrap over songs), multiple-comparison handling, and how many songs are needed for a given effect size. Include any practical guidance from music-generation evaluation papers.
9. **Recommended metric suite.** Propose one primary metric per construct (era presence, content preservation, quality, analysis attribution), 1 to 2 secondary metrics each, and the validation steps from item 7, with an implementation effort estimate in person-days and the libraries to use (fadtk, kadtk, essentia, mir_eval, librosa, pyloudnorm, others). Mark anything that needs a GPU. Then say what the qualitative listening round should check at the end (kept separate from the quantitative evaluation).
10. **Reading list.** Ten papers or resources, in order, one line each on why it matters.
