Which stem carries the era of a song, and can we regenerate it?

Course project, CommE5070 (Deep Learning for Music Analysis and Generation, NTU), music generation track. We study which stem, vocal or accompaniment, carries release-decade and release-market cues, then keep the original vocal and regenerate the accompaniment in another era's style with MusicGen-Melody. Mid-progress report. Our first project (MusicGen steering).

Which stem carries the cue

Linear probes on frozen MERT and MuQ layers, validation accuracy, six classes (chance .167). Paired tests with Holm correction.

Decade information is about as strong in the accompaniment as in the full mix; for market the vocal stem is better (MERT vocals vs accompaniment, Holm-adjusted p = .022). A second separator reproduces the pattern. A language-ID baseline alone reaches .539 on market, so language is a shortcut. Reading the pattern as language and singing style is our interpretation.

Era-targeted covers

Original vocal kept, accompaniment regenerated by MusicGen-Melody with an era prompt. Change in the target-era probability versus a neutral prompt, mean over 24 validation songs, three seeds.

PromptMERTMuQDiscogs-EffNet
1960s-0.001+0.006-0.064

  • The song stays the song: song identity from the cover alone, top-1 .892; chord agreement .336 against a shifted-chord floor of .022.
  • Production-only chains on the real accompaniment move the 2010s (+0.273) but not the 1980s (-0.036).
  • Extra prompts: 1970s +0.667 and 2000s +0.637 in expected decade (MERT); the 1960s prompt does not work.
  • Market covers are mixed: Germany and the US move with both probes, Brazil only with MuQ.
